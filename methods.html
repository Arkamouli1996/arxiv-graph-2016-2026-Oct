Methods and verification

The census covers 515,957 distinct arXiv IDs in the requested 32 mathematics categories, first submitted from 1 January 2016 through 25 October 2026 at 07:23:59 UTC. The current website uses the same reviewed snapshot as the original research dataset.

Counting, dates and categories

A paper is one base arXiv ID; revisions do not count again. First-submission timestamps are distinct from announcement dates, journal dates and revision dates. A work deposited under two different IDs is not content-deduplicated. Titles, authors and categories are current at metadata retrieval; historical changes to category assignments are not reconstructed.

Membership counting includes requested categories in all listed category tags. A paper may enter multiple fields but enters the overall membership total once. Primary-only counting includes papers primarily in these 32 fields; the overall primary total is their disjoint union. Official aliases are normalized: cs.IT to math.IT, math-ph to math.MP, stat.TH to math.ST, and cs.NA to math.NA.

Weeks, boundaries and trends

Weeks run Monday–Sunday in UTC. The graphs omit the partial first and final weeks; both remain in the dataset and evidence. Every complete week is plotted, without sampling or downsampling. The 13-week average uses the current and preceding 12 complete weeks. Pooled short-paper share divides their total short PDFs by their total measured PDFs. Prior-year weeks are retained when a rolling window crosses New Year.

Year comparisons use the same 41 complete ISO weeks (2–42) in each year, rather than comparing an unfinished year to a full year. The context-period table includes complete weeks contained wholly inside each milestone interval. These averages do not identify causal effects or adjust for seasonality and prior growth.

First-version PDF lengths

Physical version-1 PDF pages include references and appendices. Groups are inclusive: 1–5, 6–10, 11–20, 21–30, 31–40, 41–99, 100+. Of 515,957 papers, 515,921 have measured page counts; 36 remain unavailable. Unknown lengths are excluded from page groups and share denominators and are never counted as zero pages.

Lengths were measured from Cornell’s public arXiv mirror with byte-range reads, using PDFium and a validated pypdf Catalog/Pages parser. Measured objects are generation-pinned where possible. Full PDF checks agreed for all 256 pilot mirror PDFs, 63 further available mirror PDFs and 96 additional PDFs from 2016–2018. One additional recent sampled mirror object was absent; catch-up full PDFs were checked independently with PDFium and Poppler.

COVID, LLMs and interpretation

The WHO declaration and emergency end frame an emergency interval, not the beginning and end of COVID. ChatGPT launched during that interval. Long-run submission growth, field mix, author population, formatting and arXiv coverage can also change these series. The data measure submissions and PDF length, not AI authorship or research quality.

Primary-category results and a fixed 2016 field-mixture adjustment test one descriptive alternative explanation. They do not establish causality. The 2016–2018 extension supplies a longer pre-COVID baseline. Alternative date windows and models accounting for seasonality and existing trends remain appropriate next analyses.
